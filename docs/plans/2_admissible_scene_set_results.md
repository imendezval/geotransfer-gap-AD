## 1. Specify selection before searching

**Problem.** Searching only late VRU windows selected an unusually hard subset and missed ordinary moving ego tasks. A valid nine-second goal can be reached far too early even when total source motion is large. Some actors move in their full recording but stand still during the selected window.

**Solved.** The source-only catalog now records ego displacement, route goal first-entry time, per-window actor movement, nearby VRUs and moving traffic, location, recording, maneuver, and source rejection. The fixed goal-first-entry standard is step 75 or later for a step-85 goal. The source catalogs contain 193,978 unique windows; 8,424 pass the motion screen, 193,889 have complete ego source tracks, and 7,257 pass all source motion, completeness, and goal-timing requirements. Directed-road preflight passes 633 source windows. Screens search multiple location and maneuver cohorts rather than requiring a late VRU. An off-by-one source-end condition was corrected after one otherwise map-passing window failed conversion: 91 samples at 2.5 source-frame spacing require the track through `start + 225`. The rejected window remains in the ledger.

**Remaining.** The preflight is a search index, not a source-image or evaluator admission. It samples ego centers inside road lanelets and can still miss a box on pedestrian paving or a traffic island. The screened cohort and full-gate cohort are much smaller than the source catalog; their selection stages must remain visible when interpreting coverage.

Within the fixed stride-225 and stride-50 source catalogs, all **570** map-preflight-passing windows with at least one nearby moving actor have been given a conversion/prefilter screen. The 59 map-passing windows not screened have no nearby moving actor and all come from location 4. This exhausts the prespecified *screening* cohort for nearby moving traffic, but does not mean all 570 passed the expensive full evaluator gate or manual source review.

## 2. Screen route and applicable map rules

**Problem.** Lanelet2 topology and nearby-sign distance do not alone establish what the ego must do. The early screen wrongly assigned a side-street stop sign to a main-road ego in one scene, missed an oncoming turn obligation in another, and missed a slow-ego/fast-vehicle merge in location 4. A crosswalk VRU at a remote time could also create a false rule flag.

**Solved.** The rule audit inventories occupied lanelets, directed route, priority roles and lanes, referenced and nearby signs, crosswalk contacts by full ego box, VRUs, lane boundaries, omitted markings, and likely oncoming turn conflicts. The stop-sign screen now checks the ego's relation role. The crosswalk screen uses the ego-near-crosswalk source interval with a five-step margin. A general source-kinematic indicator flags a fast actor passing close to a slow ego on a converging path. It detects `r02_e169_f8764_moderate`: vehicle 174 passes at about 14.6 m/s with a 0.90 m box gap while ego 169 is near 1.1 m/s. The original rule inventory had no governing relation for this ego despite nearby DE205 sign 100261. The scene remains excluded; its individual source evidence is in `pilot/corpus/map_candidates14/r02_e169_f8764_moderate/testing/interaction_candidate_review.json`, and five excluded merge/yield candidates are compared in `pilot/audits/step2_merge_candidate_reviews.json`. The source-image review of `faithful_intersection04` resolved its recorded-window sign, priority, and crosswalk applicability under IDM.

**Remaining.** The current BehaviorBench planner observation does not carry Lanelet2 priority, sign, or crosswalk semantics. The merge candidates and several location 2 priority or VRU scenes cannot be admitted as interaction tests merely because the actor trajectories are technically preserved. The narrow kinematic indicator flagged three additional cases among an initial 64 technically viable location 4 candidates. The final scan across 121 historically technical location 4 windows found 11 with narrow or explicit-yield-lane fast-crosspath evidence. The yield-lane screen also caught `r01_e109_f7346_moderate`, whose two fast crossing vehicles were absent from the relation's listed priority lane. A wider screen without the source yield role caught unrelated adjacent traffic, confirming that manual source review remains necessary.

## 3. Review promising source recordings

**Problem.** Close actors may occupy adjacent lanes or sidewalks and never change the ego's decision. Some map-valid routes lie on pedestrian paving or painted islands. Small stationary-box overlaps may reflect annotation error, but the source aerial can itself disagree with box positions.

**Solved.** Each admitted scene has a registered source montage and a review tied to its source image and binary hash. Research review now explicitly records drivable-surface evidence at at least three source steps; claimed interactions require timed decision evidence near the actor encounter before ego terminal. Vehicle 40 beside `r00_e39_f3000_turn` was corrected from an interaction claim to adjacent traffic. Source review also identified two more location 4 straight scenes as uncontested controls. Pair-specific location 3 parked overlaps were resolved only where the aerial shows distinct parked cars; location 2 ambiguous or misregistered pairs remain excluded. A source review sheet preserves the excluded merge case rather than silently dropping it.

**Remaining.** The source image registration is an estimate, and the structural gate can check evidence presence and freshness but cannot prove the human interpretation. Several location 2 parked boxes appear metres off their visible cars, so the three-pixel overlap exception established for location 3 cannot be applied there. The latest location 1 review also rejected a technically valid cyclist near miss whose mapped ego route lies on pedestrian paving (`r15_e188_f14282_turn`), and a slow junction approach (`r10_e232_f19081_moderate`) whose action cannot safely be called uncontested before its evaluator goal terminal. Their source decision reviews are retained. More hand-reviewed interactive scenes are needed across locations and maneuvers.

## 4. Convert and apply the full Step 1 gate

**Problem.** Binary validity did not guarantee faithful closed-loop traffic. Immediate IDM can move a source-stopped leader too early; late actor disappearance, actor offroad motion, early task terminal, and counterfactual ego changes can invalidate otherwise convincing scenes.

**Solved.** The gate checks round trip, source expert replay, evaluator replay to justified ego goal terminal, parked/static actor behavior, actor lifecycle, source-scale physical boxes, route and boundaries, and one-metre ego perturbation. Hybrid startup keeps initially stopped vehicles on source replay until their source movement onset, then switches them to IDM. For `r15_e332_f25626_straight`, leader 324 remains source-timed through the stop and all 50 source-close observed frames match in the hybrid evaluator; the full gate and perturbation pass. The same hybrid switch rule passed mechanical US WOMD checks on four local samples, with reports under `pilot/audits/hybrid_us_samples/`. A gate correction now makes the **selected hybrid** trace and perturbation technically mandatory and treats immediate IDM as a diagnostic in hybrid runs. The faithful intersection pilot passes IDM research admission after source rule review, but fails the hybrid perturbation because late actor 135 disappears near steps 85–89. A current IDM rerun of 35 historically technical scenes in locations 1–3 retained all 35 technical passes, without adding a research admission.

**Remaining.** Hybrid startup is protocol-specific and does not preserve every actor in every scene. It technically repairs the following timing of `r00_e222_f14523_straight`, but that scene remains research-excluded because the nearby DE205 yield sign, crossing surface, and another vehicle near a painted island are unresolved in the source review. `r00_e81_f5721_straight` and `r02_e309_f16677_straight` lose background actors under hybrid and fail perturbation. Immediate IDM has large timing drift in several genuine following cases. A source-guided IDM speed prototype reduced one leader's step-80 error from roughly 44 m to 3.4 m but also produced large errors or actor loss elsewhere; it remains a diagnostic, outside research admission. The US checks prove mechanics only, not US/German planner training parity or comparable policy scores.

A delayed-release diagnostic waited for initially slow actors to move 1 m and reach 2 m/s before switching to IDM. Leader 76 in `r00_e81` still went offroad immediately after its later switch, and actors 301 and 306 in `r02_e309` still went offroad. Delaying the switch alone does not repair the simulator route/map discontinuity. The production hybrid protocol remains unchanged.

## 5. Build a leakage-resistant corpus

**Problem.** Overlapping windows or shared moving tracks could leak across adaptation and evaluation. Repeated screening roots and stale full-gate reports could inflate apparent corpus size.

**Solved.** `build_step2_corpus.py` deduplicates by source recording, ego, and first frame; prefers the reviewed root for the selected traffic protocol; checks time-window and moving-track independence within a recording; and assigns whole recordings to splits. Location 4 is held out. It writes separate IDM and hybrid manifests, candidate ledgers, and selection-flow summaries. Each accepted row includes ego identity, source tracks and map hashes, background hash, binary and evaluator hash, actor classes, rules, interaction claim, and split. The selection flow now distinguishes a full gate ever run from a current gate.

**Remaining.** The corpus is small, dominated by location 4 controls, and has one admitted interaction under hybrid. The intended adaptation/evaluation comparison cannot yet estimate geographic performance or failure-category frequencies. A future precision or power calculation may require more than 50 scenes.

## 6. Resolve semantic transfer for admitted interactions

**Problem.** A Lanelet2 rule in source data may be absent from GPUDrive or the planner's actual observation. This makes yield, sign, or crossing tasks unfair even when the traffic actors follow their source timing.

**Solved.** Each admission lists every inventoried rule and explains source-supported irrelevance for the recorded tested maneuver. The research gate fails if applicable priority or crosswalk conflict indicators remain, or if the manual rule review is missing or stale. The one accepted hybrid interaction is longitudinal stop-and-restart following; the side-street stop sign does not govern ego 332, and no crossing or competing priority movement controls that response. The excluded merge case shows why actor fidelity is insufficient to establish semantic fairness.

**Remaining.** A generic optional crosswalk converter now maps Lanelet2 crosswalk polygons to JSON type 18 and binary type 8 with source-coordinate round-trip checks. `r00_e365_f22735_straight` passes the technical gate with ten such crosswalks, and its pedestrian 351 crosses ahead while the source ego slows. Its research gate remains closed. In the unchanged evaluator, crosswalks in the binary do **not** reach the planner: observation comparisons at five steps change by less than 3e-6 because the C neighbor grid indexes only map types 4–6. An isolated diagnostic runtime exposing types 7–8 shows 10–13 German crosswalk segments and 1–3 segments in a nearby US WOMD scene at the same 1,151-feature observation shape; German expert and IDM lifecycle are unchanged in that runtime. This establishes a mechanical route for crosswalk features, but not fair policy use. Existing checkpoints were trained with those map features hidden, while yield priority is still unencoded and one-point US stop signs require separate handling. There is no verified matching US/German training protocol. The accepted set still cannot support yielding, sign compliance, pedestrian, or cyclist response claims. The local checkout has PPO weights, but `nvidia-smi` cannot reach an NVIDIA driver in this workspace, so new GPU training parity is not presently runnable here.

## 7. Expand interaction variety

**Problem.** Source proximity, actor count, or a short box-gap event can be mistaken for a decision opportunity. Genuine moving leader and merge cases often fail either interactive traffic fidelity or semantic transfer.

**Solved.** The search expanded across locations 1–4, turns, moderate bends, and straight travel. Framewise lane-relative geometry and aerial montages corrected the false interaction label for vehicle 40 and excluded sidewalk VRUs and painted-island maneuvers. A source-timed interaction audit requires at least five contiguous close frames after warmup, five matched frames, at least 80% match, and presence, position, gap, and speed-phase agreement. The location 1 stop-and-restart case passes under hybrid. Additional location 4 controls pass IDM, but are labeled uncontested.

**Remaining.** Neither protocol reaches ten admitted scenes with real interactions across two locations. A source-following screen of all 570 map-passing windows with moving nearby traffic found 74 possible events across 67 scenes. Sixteen had historical IDM technical passes, but none preserved its close leader over the source encounter under immediate IDM. The source-backed merge candidate is still semantically unresolved; other following cases have 7–44 m IDM drift or actor loss. A source-confirmed pedestrian-crossing candidate is now available at location 4, but its crosswalk and yield semantics are absent from the trained planner observation and it remains excluded. The accepted set does not span multiple interaction types. Do not combine protocol counts to claim a larger homogeneous benchmark.

## 8. Make research admission reproducible

**Problem.** Historical technical passes and diagnostic scores could be mistaken for research results; source, runtime, rule-audit, or manual-review changes must invalidate admission.

**Solved.** The admission contract hashes source CSVs, map files, aerial image, JSON, binary, manifest, evaluator configuration, BehaviorBench commit and dirty state, runtime, and conversion/audit code. The per-scene research gate binds manual review to current source montage, binary, rule audit, and interaction source digest. Missing, stale, and protocol-mismatched evidence fails closed. Removing the new source-surface field from an otherwise admitted review made `semantic_review_complete=false` and research admission fail; restoring the field and running the full gate passed. IDM and hybrid admissions are separate. Known failures, including the location 2 priority case, the hybrid pilot, and the newly exposed merge cases, remain excluded after reruns. The `details` shadowing bug in parked-overlap reports was corrected so exclusion reasons remain inspectable.

**Remaining.** The full candidate screen contains windows never run through the expensive full gate; the selection-flow report marks them explicitly. No planner performance score should be called a research result until the scene-set milestone, protocol parity, and later study design are satisfied.

## Experimental SMART actor input correction

The actual SMART state API supplies only active controllable agents. It omits expert background actors, including pedestrian 351 in the new crossing candidate; dormant actor headings also caused bootstrap warnings. `faithful_smart.py` now provides an experimental ego adapter that reads every present evaluator actor in stable binary order and masks absent actors. It supplies current state and the evaluator origin transform; it does not supply future source positions. With the corrected crosswalk binary, the adapter produced finite actions over 81 expert-replay steps, included pedestrian 351 at the decision, and matched evaluator actor identity at every prediction refresh. A US WOMD run completed ten steps with finite actions and no identity mismatch at prediction refresh. These are observation and inference diagnostics, not policy-controlled closed-loop validation or research admission. The adapter still needs evaluator integration, counterfactual observation tests, and hash-bound planner-specific admission.
